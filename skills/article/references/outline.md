# Outline guidance

A good outline is the difference between a post that ranks and a pile of paragraphs.
Build it around what the searcher needs to do, not around the keyword string.

## Shape

- One introduction and one conclusion.
- H2 sections in between; H3s only to break down a dense H2.
- An optional FAQ before the conclusion.
- Body H2 count scales with length: micro and short 2 to 3, medium about 4, long
  about 5, very-long 6 or more. The FAQ and conclusion come on top of these.

## Rules

- Organize around intent: the subtopics, comparisons, objections, examples, and steps
  the reader needs to act. Sections shouldn't overlap, and together they should leave
  no obvious gap.
- Lead with the reader's problem and the highest-value sections. Don't bury the answer
  under history or definitions.
- Put the primary keyword, or a natural variant, in at least one heading. Spread
  related terms across other headings, and drop any that would make a heading sound
  robotic.
- Headings name the payoff of the section. Phrase a heading as the question a reader
  would ask only where that is how people actually search for it; a whole outline of
  questions reads like a FAQ.
- Mark where the information gain lives: the section that carries the original
  example, number, test, or opinion the ranking pages don't have. If no section does,
  the outline isn't finished.
- Size sections to the budget. Roughly 200 to 350 words per H2, or 120 to 220 on micro
  and short posts. The total word budget beats any per-section floor. Split a section
  that runs long; merge one that runs thin.

## Per-section notes

Attach a one-line intent note to each heading: what the reader should leave that
section knowing. Drafting uses these to stay on point and to avoid repeating itself
across sections.
