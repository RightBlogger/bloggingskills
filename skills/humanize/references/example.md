# Worked example: humanizing a slopped paragraph

One annotated rewrite, because the hard part isn't spotting a single tell, it's
untangling a paragraph where they stack. Read this before a heavy de-slop.

## Before (almost every sentence is a tell)

> In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, choosing the right project management
> tool isn't just important, it's absolutely critical. Let's dive in. The truth is
> simple: most teams are drowning. Not in work. Not in deadlines. In tools. Here's the
> kicker: the very software meant to help is quietly suffocating their productivity.
> Studies show that teams waste countless hours every week. It's worth noting that this
> is a pervasive issue. Think of it as a tax on every single thing you do. So what's the
> solution? A unified platform. From scrappy startups to sprawling enterprises,
> organizations everywhere are leveraging robust, cutting-edge solutions to streamline
> their workflows and unlock their team's full potential. In conclusion, the right tool
> can be a genuine game-changer.

What's wrong, in order of damage:

- Banned opener ("In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape").
- Negative parallelism and fragments ("isn't just important, it's critical"; "Not in
  work. Not in deadlines. In tools.").
- Pedagogical and false-suspense filler ("Let's dive in", "Here's the kicker", "The
  truth is simple", "It's worth noting").
- Magic adverbs and stakes inflation ("quietly suffocating", "absolutely critical").
- Vague attribution and filler quantity ("Studies show", "countless hours").
- Self-answered question ("So what's the solution? A unified platform.").
- Patronizing analogy ("Think of it as a tax").
- False range ("From scrappy startups to sprawling enterprises").
- AI-ese pileup ("leveraging robust, cutting-edge solutions to streamline ... unlock ...
  full potential").
- Signposted conclusion and a banned noun ("In conclusion", "game-changer").

Because most sentences are tells, this is a Scope case (Method step 5): rewrite from the
argument, don't patch sentence by sentence. The argument is small and true: teams lose
time to tool sprawl, and consolidating onto one platform helps.

## After (same length, same claim, human)

> Picking a project management tool is one of those decisions that quietly shapes how a
> team spends its days. Get it wrong and the cost compounds.
>
> Most teams aren't buried by their workload. They're buried by the tools meant to handle
> it. The software that promised to speed things up ends up slowing everyone down: an
> hour lost switching apps, another hunting for a file. Over a week that adds up, and it
> works like a tax on everything the team is trying to ship.
>
> The fix is usually consolidation. Startups and large companies alike are moving their
> work onto one platform instead of stitching five together. When the tool fits, the
> change in how a team operates is hard to overstate.

What changed, and why:

- Kept the one real idea (tool sprawl wastes time; consolidating helps) and the one
  concrete image (the "tax"), since that was the draft's best line. Cut only the "Think
  of it as" frame around it.
- Removed every banned phrase, both em dashes, the false range, and the AI-ese stack.
- Replaced "Studies show countless hours" with a concrete, honest picture ("an hour lost
  switching apps") instead of a fake statistic. If a real number existed it would go
  here; since none was given, the rewrite doesn't invent one. When a fact you need is
  missing, flag it to the user rather than launder it into vagueness.
- Varied sentence openings and lengths so no rhythm repeats.

Note: "quietly" survives once, where it does real work rather than inflating. One
instance is fine. The original's problem was the pile-up, not any single word.
