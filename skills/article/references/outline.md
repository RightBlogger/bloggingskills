# Outline guidance

A good outline is the difference between a post that ranks and a pile of paragraphs.
Build it around search intent, not around the keyword string.

## Shape

- Exactly one introduction and one conclusion.
- H2 sections in between; H3s only to break down a dense H2.
- Optional FAQ before the conclusion.
- H2 count scales with length: short ~3, medium ~4, long ~5, very-long ~6+.

## Rules

- Organize around what the searcher actually wants: the subtopics, comparisons,
  objections, examples, and steps they need to act. Cover the question fully. Sections
  shouldn't overlap, and together they should leave no obvious gap.
- Put the primary keyword, or a natural variant, in at least one heading. Spread
  related terms across different headings. Don't cram them into one, and don't force
  any that would make a heading sound robotic.
- Headings describe the payoff of the section. No "Introduction", "Overview", or
  "More information".
- Each H2 should hold roughly 200 to 350 words. On micro and short posts, run
  sections shorter (120 to 220 words) and use two or three H2s so the whole piece
  still lands in range. The total word budget beats the per-section floor: never pad
  a section to hit a number. If a section runs much longer, split it; much shorter,
  merge it.
- Lead with the reader's problem and the highest-value sections. Don't bury the
  answer under history or definitions.

## Per-section notes

Attach a one-line intent note to each heading: what the reader should leave that
section knowing. The drafting step uses these to stay on point and to avoid repeating
itself across sections.
