# Intro, section, conclusion, and FAQ rules

The full style contract is in house-style's ai-tells.md. These are the
section-specific rules drafting leans on most.

## Introduction

- 3–5 sentences, under 150 words, in 2–3 short paragraphs. Never one block.
- Shape: hook, then context, then a transition into the body. Lead with the reader's
  problem.
- Make the bottom line clear in the first few sentences.
- Banned openers: "In today's rapidly evolving world", "It's no secret that", "In the
  ever-changing landscape of", "As we navigate the complexities of", "Whether you're a
  seasoned pro or just starting out". No dictionary definitions. No "In this article
  you'll learn: 1, 2, 3".

## Body sections

- 200 to 350 words under each H2, shorter on micro and short posts. Mix sentence
  lengths; keep most under 20 words.
- Hold the running draft in mind. Never restate a point an earlier section made.
- Lean concrete: real examples, named tools, specific numbers. If a sentence could
  appear verbatim in any competitor's article, sharpen it or cut it.
- Prose first. Use a list only for a genuine sequence or quick reference, and no more
  than two or three lists in the whole post.

## Conclusion

- Its own H2, the final section. Reinforce the single strongest takeaway; don't recap
  every point.
- Connect back to the opening hook.
- Add a CTA only if it genuinely fits. For informational or analytical posts, skip it.
- Banned closers: "In conclusion", "To sum up", "All in all", "At the end of the day".
  Deliver the closing thought directly.

## FAQ (optional)

- 3–5 questions a real person would type into Google or an AI assistant.
- Each question is an H3. Answers are 2–4 sentences, answer-first.
- Don't restate the question. Vary how answers begin; not every one starts with "Yes"
  or "No". No horizontal rules between questions.
