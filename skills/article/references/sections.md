# Intro, section, conclusion, and FAQ rules

The full style contract is the house-style rulebook (ai-tells.md). These are the
section-level rules drafting leans on most.

## Introduction

- 3 to 5 sentences, under 150 words, in 2 or 3 short paragraphs.
- Hook, then context, then a turn into the body. Lead with the reader's problem and
  give the bottom line in the first few sentences, since many readers (and answer
  engines) take only the opening.
- The rulebook lists the stock openers to skip.

## Body sections

- Open each section with its answer or main point in one or two plain sentences that
  make sense out of context. Support it after. That opening is what skimmers read and
  what AI answers quote.
- Hold the running draft in mind. Never restate a point an earlier section made.
- Lean concrete: real examples, named tools, specific numbers with their source. If a
  sentence could appear verbatim in a competitor's article, sharpen it or cut it.
- Prose first. Use a list only for a genuine sequence or quick reference, and keep it
  to two or three lists in the whole post. A table earns its place for side-by-side
  comparisons.

## Conclusion

- Its own H2, the final section, under a heading that says something.
- Give the single strongest takeaway or the reader's next step. Don't recap every
  point.
- Connect back to the opening hook.
- Add a CTA only if it fits. For informational or analytical posts, skip it.

## FAQ (optional)

Add one only when real reader questions remain that the body didn't answer. Don't
repeat body content as questions.

- 3 to 5 questions a real person would type into Google or an AI assistant.
- Each question is an H3. Answers are 2 to 4 sentences, answer first.
- Don't restate the question, and vary how answers begin.
